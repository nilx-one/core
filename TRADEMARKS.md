# 0x1 Product Identity and Trademark Policy

**Status:** initial policy; it does not claim that every listed mark is registered in every jurisdiction.

The repository license does not grant permission to present a derivative product as official 0x1.

## Protected Project Identity

The project reserves control over product identity associated with:

- `0x1`;
- `nilx.one`;
- `nilx-one` where used as project or organization identity;
- official 0x1 and nilx.one logos, emblems, wordmarks, and visual identity;
- confusingly similar names or presentation that imply official status, sponsorship, endorsement, or canonical compatibility.

## Allowed Referential Use

Truthful referential statements are allowed where applicable law permits them. A third party may state that software interoperates with 0x1, derives from published 0x1 source, or is being evaluated against 0x1, provided the statement does not imply an official relationship that does not exist.

## Forks and Derivatives

A fork or derivative permitted by its source license must use its own product identity unless the project steward grants explicit permission otherwise.

Changing protocol semantics and continuing to market the result as canonical `0x1` is not permitted by this identity policy.

A derivative must not reuse official logos or presentation in a way that makes users reasonably believe they are using the canonical application or service.

## Compatibility Language

Technical compatibility and product-identity permission are separate. Passing tests or interoperating with 0x1 does not by itself grant permission to call a derivative `official`, `canonical`, or otherwise imply endorsement.

## Permissions

The project may grant written permission for integrations, distributions, research, events, partnerships, or other uses. Permission may be scoped and may be revised according to its terms.
